Safety Guidelines

Core Rules: Do not cross restricted-access highways or freeways, never use your phone to take notes or photos while standing on a road, and always leave immediately if asked by a property owner, business, police officer, or resident.

Roadway & Traffic Safety

  • Stick to sidewalks and car-free paths when possible.
  • If you must walk on the road, walk on the left side (so cars are coming towards you), and stick as far to the left as possible, ideally in the shoulder if it is present (this is safer than walking on the right because you can see cars approaching).
  • If a road has a higher speed limit and no shoulders or sidewalks, you should note this, and either walk in grass if safe—or simply turn around and drive to the next destination.
  • Do not cross restricted-access highways and freeways or walk alongside them.

Visibility & Phone Use

  • Wearing at least one item of clothing that is fluorescent green or blaze orange (or to a slightly lesser degree, with neon colors) with reflective elements is recommended.
  • Keep alert for cars and bicycles and do not use your phone to take notes or pictures on roads—wait until you are safely on a car-free surface like sidewalks, grass, or pedestrian walkways.
  • Wearable blinking lights are also good for visibility. These guidelines are even more important at dusk, dawn, twilight, and nighttime.

Off-Road & Weather Conditions

  • If you choose to go off-road along a nonpaved trail, take precautions to not trip on roots and other hazards and keep alert for snakes and insects.
  • If you expect to go off-piste, wear proper clothing and footwear.
  • If you encounter a water crossing without a bridge, document it with a note/photo and then turn around. Refrain from fording bodies of water.
  • Do not do missions during flooded conditions or when extreme weather is present.

Property Boundaries & Encounters

Obey signage, fencing, and local laws, and document paths that are marked as private property or only for neighborhood residents. We do not condone trespassing, although we acknowledge that sometimes property lines, ownership, and permission of passage can be murky.

  • If you encounter a property owner, agent of a place of business, police, or neighborhood resident asking you to leave—comply and once back on public rights-of-way or property, document the encounter and area in which it occurred.
  • When in doubt, check the Regrid app before entering. Use your discretion and observe context clues. Purple lines on trees can signify "posted" in some states, so keep that in mind.
  • Some adjacent landowners may react negatively to anyone near their property taking photos or believe that they own adjacent public parcels that you're exploring—do not challenge them on this or take any additional photos and make note of the encounter only once removed from the situation.
  • Keep a respectful distance from any houses or structures if encountered and do not photograph homes.
