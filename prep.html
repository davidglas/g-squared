Volunteer Quick Start Guide

Mission

  • Task: To map and document roads, sidewalks, paths (both formal and informal), and signage.
  • Actions: Walking/jogging/running around specified areas, using a device to log GPS track, taking pictures and notes, dropping pins and drawing lines in an app, and emailing data to the volunteer coordinator once data is gathered.
  • Reason: To help make more accurate maps while facilitating urban planning and pedestrian/cyclist advocacy efforts.

Equipment

A smartphone with an app for GPS track making + an app for field mapping. If you have a GPS watch, you can log the activity in your watch, while using the field mapping app for notes as well.

Software

Logging

  • Duck Tracker (free; simple-to-use app; recommended)
  • GPX Tracker (similar to above)
  • Strava (need to use desktop website to export GPS)

Field Mapping

  • Georaf Landplot (free; simple-to-use app; recommended)

Optional

  • Regrid (free; useful for seeing property ownership of parcels)

Note: There are likely other apps that can do the job, but these are the most user-friendly and privacy-centric apps I've tested (on iOS at least). Feel free to use what you want as long as you're getting a GPX trail and geotagged notes/photos as a result. You can track your GPS trail with Landplot, but you can't also take notes at the same time and it doesn't export GPX files.

Important: Read safety guidelines before heading out.

Tutorial

  1. Contact volunteer coordinator with information on your location (general area of the city or zip code is fine—I just don't want to send you too far out of your way), what transportation options you have access to, and the number of hours you generally are looking to volunteer. I'll send you some options of areas that fit the org's criteria for you to pick from within your vicinity. You must fill out the volunteer waiver before participation.
  2. Travel to the specified area at a time convenient for you. Make sure to block out a little extra time in case you end up having to walk back a bit further than expected. If you drive, make sure to park somewhere on the street that is safe and legal (and preferably courteous to the nearby residents as well) to park.
  3. Start a new activity in Strava/Duck Tracker...etc. or your watch. Start recording. It is important for timekeeping and data integrity purposes that you keep recording on during the exploration—if you find that recording has stopped, try to restart it before continuing.
  4. Walk through the area, sticking to sidewalks and pedestrian walkways if possible. Use an app such as Landplot or OSM Maps to drop pins with notes on areas of significance and take some photos if anything sticks out.
  5. Make sure to document any desire paths, suspected cut-throughs, informal trails, and features that aren't already present on the map you received. The best way to document these is to walk along them while your GPS is recording. When taking notes on paths, remember to document surface type, ease of following, and any hazards.
  6. Export your GPX tracks and other notes/features and email them to the volunteer coordinator, along with a note of how long you were working and any concerns or comments you think important to include in your report.
